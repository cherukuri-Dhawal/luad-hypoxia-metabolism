Tumor hypoxia and metabolic gene expression

We calculated a gene-expression-based hypoxia score for each selected primary tumor, explored metabolic associations, compared six prediction models and analyzed overall survival.

What the model predicts: an RNA-derived high/low hypoxia group, not measured oxygen, treatment response or an individual clinical outcome. Independent patient-cohort validation has not been performed.

1. Data and hypoxia scoring

The public Xena TPM matrix was downloaded directly and its metadata verified as log₂(TPM + 1). We selected the lexicographically first primary tumor sample per patient, averaged duplicate-symbol log-expression rows, and retained a broad fixed background for scoring. All original data and exclusions are audited in the CSV and JSON outputs.

Hallmark Hypoxia contains 200 symbols; 199 matched directly and HACL2 was omitted without an unverified alias substitution. GSEApy ssGSEA used within-sample ranks and exponent 0.25. We use raw ES, not the cohort-rescaled NES, so another patient's presence does not change a patient's score. A single-sample invariance check passed.

Descriptive tertile groups: {'Low': 172, 'High': 172, 'Intermediate': 172}. For prediction, cutoffs are learned within training data and frozen before application to test patients.

2. Exploration and clustering

PCA, ICA, t-SNE and UMAP are exploratory views of variable genes outside the hypoxia-scoring set. They are not model validation. Clustering uses 20 PCA components; the two plotted axes are only a visualization. K was selected using silhouette score rather than agreement with the hypoxia labels.

Additional outputs include ICA and t-SNE plots, clustering metrics for K=2–8, hierarchical clustering and DBSCAN, seed stability, and example distribution/QQ checks.

3. Gene and metabolic pathway associations

We tested 23,710 adequately expressed genes using Welch tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction; Mann–Whitney tests provide a sensitivity comparison. A descriptive threshold of adjusted p < 0.05 and absolute difference in mean log₂(TPM + 1) ≥ 0.5 identified 2,137 differing genes outside the scoring set, including 80 genes in the chosen metabolic reference sets.

These are descriptive full-cohort associations. Hypoxia is itself defined from the same expression experiment, so differences do not establish oxygen causation or metabolic flux. 4. Prediction on unseen patients

We reserved 104 patients before predictive fitting. The remaining 412 patients formed the development cohort. Six classifiers were compared using five outer development folds and three-fold inner tuning. All gene filtering, scaling and supervised feature selection were fitted within training folds. Predictors were drawn only from the nine prespecified metabolic/related gene sets, excluding every hypoxia-scoring gene.

Selected model: LogisticRegression. It was selected using development CV only, then fitted on 276 development patients. Its frozen training-derived cutoffs were applied to the untouched test cohort; intermediate test patients were excluded according to this predeclared extreme-group task.

5. Overall survival

Survival event coding and time units were checked against clinical vital status and follow-up/death days. The analysis includes 503 patients with positive follow-up and 182 deaths. The three-group log-rank p-value is 0.0001062.

The exploratory Cox model used 485 complete cases, adjusting for age, sex and categorical stage, with a small regularization penalty (0.01). Hypoxia hazard ratio per one score standard deviation: 1.272 (95% CI 1.096–1.476; p=0.001578). A hazard ratio above 1 indicates higher estimated hazard under the fitted model; it is not a patient-specific survival prediction.

Proportional-hazards check for hypoxia: p=0.3781. This check did not flag a hypoxia-specific violation at p<0.05; inspect all covariates and diagnostics in the CSV outputs. Missingness and clinical/treatment confounding remain limitations.

Survival sensitivity check: age/sex/stage-adjusted model diagnostics flagged sex and stage III proportional-hazards assumptions. A stage-and-sex-stratified Cox sensitivity analysis is supplied in the CSV outputs; interpret the simple hazard ratio as exploratory.
